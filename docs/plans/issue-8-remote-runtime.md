### Implementation notes after independent review (2026-10-24)

The quiet-pause and agent-cgroup slices exist in source and are unit-tested (see ADR 0030). Two limits are recorded here so no later reading overstates them, and neither is waived:

- **Transport loss is not retry-preserving.** A pause that is delivered preserves `attempts`, the owner-input rounds, the workspace and the repository. A pause that cannot be delivered (an outage, or a coordinator without the route) is not reported as a failure and does not re-prompt, but the coordinator then applies ordinary lease expiry: `attempts + 1`, an unconfirmed-stop slot, and with the default budget of one a second expiry fails and orphans the job. Preserving the budget across transport loss needs a reviewed route by which the coordinator learns of the pause, and remains a required implementation and runtime blocker.
- **A submitted prompt that was never seen to settle retains ownership; it does not pause.** An unobserved pickup is not a demonstrably never-started turn: the agent may still start after the grace, so releasing the slot or the host writer lock under it would allow a second job beside a writer. A stalled prompt with no pickup observed, or an idle reading with no settle behind it, therefore ends as the orphan `orphan_prompt_unsettled`, keeping the repository, the slot and the writer lock for the owner. Its wording says only that submitted work was not observed to settle; it claims neither that the agent is working nor any cause, and infers no quota or sign-in state. Only a turn that WAS observed to settle `idle` or `done` without a valid result (and a prompt withheld by the memory guard before anything was sent) pauses, retry-preservingly when the pause is delivered. The same rule governs a timeout or cancellation: `cancel()` releases only on a positive recorded-identity idle or done proof, never while a prompt's turn is unsettled or `runJob` may still dispatch. This supersedes the earlier planning sentence and review bullet that described a stalled turn as pausing.
- **Neither slice is live evidence.** No real Ducky agent, provider response or pause has been observed.

### Implementation notes: durable pause delivery (2026-10-24, run 44b45e6f35)

Implemented in two commits (protocol and persistence; executor and recovery), unit-, on-disk- and HTTP-tested, never run with a real agent. See ADR 0030 for the design and its limits. Recorded here so no later reading overstates them:

- **Deferral is for negotiated leases only, and is not a grace.** A lease that negotiated `pause-delivery-v1` with a recorded workspace is not charged at expiry; the job waits holding every hold, and the reservation timer skips it. Its exits are the original executor's report, that executor's attested recovery claim (the ordinary charge, once) and the owner's cancel (an orphan, cleared by `/job cleanup`). Another executor or host, or the same one without the attestation, cannot claim it, charge it, exhaust it or supersede it. Peers that did not negotiate keep ordinary charging.
- **Durable dispatch provenance replaced in-memory turn proof.** `dispatching` is journaled before every prompt (no record, no prompt) and `settled` after an observed settle; recovery, cancel and timeout, an owner resume's prompt and the fresh proof read it. Absent, unreadable, mismatched or `dispatching` is an orphan with all holds, even if a fresh read is idle. This corrects the review-identified defect that a restart or a recovery claim could release on an unobserved prompt.
- **Executor state is new, explicit and private.** `DUCKY_EXECUTOR_STATE_DIR` is required with no default, refused under temp or runtime paths, 0700/0600, atomic, bounded, fail-closed on any untrusted record and never aged out. An intent is forgotten only on a validated, identity-matched terminal answer; a held intent withholds claims, turns and prompts (loop, runner and orchestrator).
- **Both report paths enforce the job-owned slot,** and the original deadline is preserved through `waiting_for_executor`.
- **Residual limits, not waived:** a crash before the intent is durable; a lost executor state directory or host; an older coordinator (held, not downgraded; a coordinated-upgrade limit) and an older executor (ordinary accounting); jobs in flight at deployment have no journal and orphan; a coordinator restart's first tick still expires leases at once (no post-start grace was added; the negotiated path makes that harmless and it is tested against a real on-disk database).
